write a github project for a home assistant integration which fulfils all the coding standards for quality, as set by home assistant.

The integration must track the savings which result from running solar panels at home, also in combination with dynamic tariffs with different import/export tariffs which change dynamically over time.

the idea is that the generated solar energy first reduces the imports from the grid, then when more energy is produced than needed, the excess is exported to the grid, at typically a much lower value.

The integration should take as input one energy sensor tracking energy generation by solar panels. This sensor can track total or daily production, but it is expected to provide an always increasing value, therefore it needs to filter out negative differences such as those resulting from tracking daily energy generation which resets at midnight.

The integration should also take two sensors for imports: imported energy and the price of said imported energy.
The integration should also take two more sensors for exports: amount of exports and the value of said exports.

The entities from the smart meter tracking imports and exports are not updated at the same time as the energy generation from solar panels: the former are updated likely every 15 seconds, the latter once every 5 minutes.

At each update of the sensors tracking energy generated, the integration should first calculate net export as the diffrence exports minus imports. If negative, no net export takes place and the net export should be zero. If positive, the revenue from exports should be valued using the sensor entity for value of exported energy. The net exports should be accumulated into a temporary variable.

Once the sensor for solar generation is updated, the accumulated net exported energy shoud be subtracted from it, then the remaining generated energy should be valued using the sensor tracking value of imported energy.

The two calculated revenues should be exposed as two sensors using the appropriate unit of measure of currency. The total revenue, obtained as sum of the two separate revenues, should also be exposed as a sensor.

Be sure to add the correct definitions so that Home assistant can track statistical data about the sensors exposed.

The project should also comprise test cases which can be run during future development to ensure the results are consistent and no regressions take place.

--- Amendment: self-consumption from the energy balance ---

The rule above values solar generation by first computing net export as
"exports minus imports" at every reading and subtracting the accumulated
result from generation. That is an approximation. Assuming all exported
energy originates from the solar panels, conservation of energy over any
window gives

    solar + import = load + export

so the generation that served the house is exactly

    self consumption = solar generated - exported energy

The imported energy register does not appear, so the integration no longer
asks for an imported-energy sensor; the import price sensor is still needed
to value self-consumed energy. Unlike the net-export rule, this expression
uses only monotonically increasing counters, so it is additive: it gives the
same answer whether it is evaluated at every reading or once at the end of an
accounting period. The net-export rule instead clamps at zero per reading,
which overstates self-consumption whenever both registers advance within the
same reading, and overstates it further the longer the evaluation period.

--- Amendment: battery tracking and counterfactual scenarios ---

The configuration gains an optional bottom section with three more entities:
energy imported from the grid, energy charged into the battery, and energy
taken from the battery. They are selected all together or not at all.

When they are present, the calculation is expressed as three scenarios, each
one a grid position valued with the same two price entities:

1) the actual cost/revenue, from the import and export registers as they are;
2) the hypothetical cost without a battery, obtained by correcting the actual
   grid position for what the battery contributed, "discharge - charge". The
   correction is applied against the opposite direction of the actual flow
   first, so energy the battery supplied cancels export before it forces an
   import, and energy it absorbed cancels import before it becomes export;
3) the hypothetical cost without a battery and without the panels, obtained by
   correcting the second scenario for the generation as well.

The published savings are differences between the scenarios: battery savings
is 2) minus 1), solar savings is 3) minus 2), and total savings is 3) minus 1),
which is their sum. All of them can be negative, because exporting at a
negative price and charging a battery from the grid both cost money.

The scenario split runs on every change of any energy register, which keeps
the correction accurate: over a longer window an import and an export can
cancel each other out before the correction is applied. Valuing the split
energy still happens once per accounting interval, or at a tariff change,
because money is linear in energy while a tariff holds.

A battery register with no usable value holds the split instead of distorting
it: the observed deltas wait, and the split is exact once the register reports
again. The wait is bounded at five minutes, after which the held energy is
accounted for as if the battery had been idle and a warning is logged.

The same algorithm covers a system with no battery, as scenarios 1) and 3):
the battery correction is zero, so scenario 2) collapses onto scenario 1) and
battery savings are zero, while solar savings reduce exactly to the
energy-balance rule of the previous amendment,

    self consumption = solar generated - exported energy

valued at the import price, plus the exported energy valued at the export
price. Both cases therefore share one code path. The one figure a system
without the import register cannot produce is the absolute cost of a scenario;
the differences between scenarios do not contain it, so the savings stay exact
and only the scenario cost entities are withheld.

The scenario costs and the virtual "no battery" and "no battery, no solar"
energy meters are published as entities that are disabled by default, so only
users who want them pay for them, and they can be seeded or corrected with the
same set_value action as the savings totals.

--- Correction: the attribution window ---

Attributing the scenarios on every change of any energy register, as the
amendment above prescribed, is wrong in practice. The battery correction is
cancelled against the grid flow of the same window, and the registers do not
report together: a smart meter reports every few seconds while a battery
counter reports about once a minute. Almost every window then holds grid
export with no battery delta, which is read as solar export, and the
occasional window holds a battery delta with no grid export, which is read as
an avoided import. A battery selling to the grid overnight showed up as solar
export revenue on a night with no sun, of exactly the amount
"grid export * export price".

Attribution therefore happens once per settlement window, over the same energy
the settlement is about to value. Because the window now has to hold a reading
from every meter, a battery entry settles at no less than sixty seconds,
whatever the configured accounting interval says. Measured against recorded
history, the spurious solar savings of an evening of battery export fall from
the full export revenue to under one percent of it, and the result is stable
for any window between thirty seconds and an hour.
